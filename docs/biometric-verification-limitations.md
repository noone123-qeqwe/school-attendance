# Face verification limits and privacy review

## Technical limits

Student Settings separates camera face registration from fingerprint/device credential registration. The camera flow captures front, slightly left, and slightly right views. The server validates all three together for image quality, opposite head turns, and pairwise SFace similarity before atomically replacing the enrollment. The head-turn check projects YuNet's nose landmark onto the eye line; it is a conservative 2D proxy, not a measured 3D pose or PAD. Real-device trials must assess its acceptance rate across students, cameras, lighting, and normal facial variation before relying on this workflow.

All three normalized JPEG references are encrypted together in the existing private face-photo record. Changing the public avatar does not overwrite a camera enrollment. Legacy single-photo enrollments remain readable. Attendance comparison uses the strongest enrolled view for each live sample, while every live sample must still meet the unchanged configured similarity threshold and existing frame-consistency checks. Additional references can change false acceptance and rejection rates, so calibrate the complete workflow, not just a single-photo comparison. No external verification or provider setting is enabled by this change. Fingerprint registration uses WebAuthn; the operating system chooses the available verification method and may offer a PIN instead of a fingerprint.

## Guided camera attendance

The browser uses MediaPipe for local guidance and captures front, left, right, up, and down automatically. The server randomizes the order of the four turns, checks the centered transitions and each stable hold with YuNet, applies the Open Model Zoo [anti-spoof-mn3 RGB classifier](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/open_model_zoo/tree/master/models/public/anti-spoof-mn3) to every frame, then compares the weakest identity result with the existing encrypted camera references using SFace. An ordinary profile avatar cannot substitute for camera enrollment. The browser cannot submit a trusted score or verdict. Attendance is recorded only after both checks and any school-configured device checks pass.

The challenge is single-use, time-limited, and tied to the account, browser session, and QR value. Exact repeated samples and recent identical frame sets are rejected. These checks cannot establish camera provenance on a compromised browser and do not guarantee protection against reordered video, virtual cameras, real-time synthesis, or all physical presentation attacks. No depth or infrared data is used. See [NIST's discussion of PAD and injection attacks](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html).

Camera samples are sent to the school server over HTTPS. The PHP matcher writes private temporary files for OpenCV and removes them in a `finally` cleanup. Cleanup after a process or host failure is best effort. Verification keeps no video, submitted frames, or embeddings in attendance records or application logs. The application keeps a keyed digest of each recent frame set for ten minutes and logs the outcome without the images. The enrolled reference remains encrypted.

### Rollout and validation

The Docker build pins the PAD model and the browser detector assets. Keep `FACE_ATTENDANCE_LIVENESS_ENABLED=false` until the school completes its privacy review and tests the full workflow with consenting adults on representative desktop and mobile cameras. The feature flag enables the admin consent form. Super administrators record documented adult student or verified guardian consent on the student detail page. Students can withdraw camera attendance consent from the camera notice. The camera path requires both current consent and an existing camera enrollment. Registered device verification and the teacher's QR roster **Mark Present** action provide alternatives.

Before enabling attendance use, measure genuine acceptance and attack rejection across front lighting, dim and backlit rooms, low resolution and high resolution cameras, and different devices. Include printed photos, photos and videos on phones and tablets, identical and reordered uploads, virtual camera feeds, and mismatched students. Record counts and outcomes without retaining student recordings. The initial model smoke test rejected two public spoof examples and also rejected the public live example. Treat this as an indication that real-device acceptance and thresholds need validation, not evidence of deployment readiness.

## School privacy review required

The school, as the data controller, should have its Data Protection Officer or qualified privacy counsel determine and document the lawful basis for this specific attendance use before enabling face verification. That review should cover notice, purpose limitation, proportionality, access controls, retention/deletion for profile photos and temporary processing artifacts, student/parent rights, vendor roles, and a practical non-face alternative. The application notice is not a substitute for the school's privacy notice or legal-basis decision.

The National Privacy Commission's published material says biometric processing remains subject to the Data Privacy Act and its implementing rules, and emphasizes transparency, legitimate purpose, proportionality, and security measures. See the [NPC advisory on biometric data processing](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/NPC-Advisory-Opinion-No.-2025-007_Redacted.pdf), the [Data Privacy Act of 2012](https://privacy.gov.ph/data%20-privacy-act/), and the NPC's [right to be informed guidance](https://privacy.gov.ph/the-right-to-be-informed/).
